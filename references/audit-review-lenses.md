# Audit Review Lenses

How to review a draft without turning review into a fixed ritual. A lens
is a scope of scrutiny, not a mandatory step. Select the lenses that fit
the risk, the report type, the audience, and the stage of the work.

Do not use twenty reviewers on the same scope. Use a small number of
independent lenses, each with a clear question and a recorded result.

## Lens menu

| Lens | Core question | Typical output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence and numbers | Does every material number map to a source, period, denominator, and unit? | Number-to-source exceptions |
| Cause and effect | Is the causal chain complete, and are alternatives addressed? | Causal exceptions |
| Conclusion and confidence | Does the evidence support the conclusion and the stated confidence? | Conclusion exceptions |
| Recommendations and closure | Is each action owned, feasible, measurable, and evidence-based? | Closure exceptions |
| Decision support | Can the reader decide from the report alone? | Decision gaps |
| Language and readability | Is the prose clear, neutral, and consistent? | Language exceptions |
| Presentation and print | Do tables, figures, and pages work in the intended medium? | Layout exceptions |
| Independence and standalone | Does the report stand alone, and is the source independence clear? | Independence gaps |
| Package integrity | Are all artefacts the same version and hash? | Integrity exceptions |
| Ethics and confidentiality | Is the report fair, necessary, and safe to distribute? | Ethics/confidentiality exceptions |
| Regulatory or contractual | Does the report meet the applicable form and content requirements? | Compliance exceptions |

## Selecting lenses

Choose by:

- **Risk**: a high-impact conclusion needs more lenses than a routine
  update.
- **Report type**: a regulatory filing needs a compliance lens; a board
  memo needs a decision-support lens.
- **Audience**: a non-specialist reader needs a language and
  jargon-translation lens.
- **Stage**: an early draft needs evidence and causal lenses; a final
  draft needs package and print lenses.
- **Novelty**: a new methodology or a new data source needs an
  independent re-performance lens.

Record why a lens was included or excluded. "We did not review
recommendations" is a decision, not an omission.

## Stakeholder perspectives

A lens can be run from a stakeholder's point of view:

- the decision-maker who needs the conclusion and the ask;
- the non-specialist who needs the consequence, not the mechanism;
- the executive who needs the conditions and the stop points;
- the auditee who needs a fair, specific, and actionable finding;
- the technical specialist who needs the evidence and the uncertainty;
- the next reviewer who needs the source trail;
- the external reader who has only the report.

Do not ask every stakeholder to read the whole report. Ask each one the
question their role must answer.

## Adversarial checks

### Reverse the conclusion

For each material conclusion, ask:

- What evidence would make this conclusion false or weaker?
- Did we look for that evidence?
- If we found it, how did we address it?
- If we did not find it, why not?

If the team cannot state a falsification condition, the conclusion is
written too strongly.

### Simulate publication and challenge

Assume the report has been published and a knowledgeable critic
challenges it. Identify:

- the number most likely to be disputed;
- the cause that is least well supported;
- the impact most likely to be called exaggerated;
- the recommendation least likely to be implementable;
- the appendix least able to defend the body.

Fix those before release, not after.

### Test unintended consequences

Ask whether a recommendation could:

- add a procedure without removing the bottleneck;
- shift responsibility without granting authority;
- increase reporting volume without improving data quality;
- create an incentive to improve the metric rather than the outcome;
- delay the work without reducing the risk.

A recommendation that creates a new risk is not a complete
recommendation.

## Recording results

Use a consistent record:

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Lens | Which scope was reviewed |
| Reviewer | Who reviewed it |
| Scope | What was in and out |
| Result | Pass, fail, or conditional |
| Evidence | The specific page, number, or section reviewed |
| Exception | What is wrong or missing |
| Action | What must change and who owns it |

Do not let a reviewer silently rewrite the conclusion. The reviewer
records the exception; the owner decides and documents the change.

## Three tests

### Appendix-dependency test

If the reader needs an appendix to understand the main conclusion, the
body fails.

### Falsifiability test

If no evidence could weaken the conclusion, the conclusion is not
empirical. Rewrite it with a condition that would change it.

### Feasibility test

If the recommendation cannot be implemented, afforded, or accepted by
the named owner, it fails. Rewrite it as a decision, a condition, or a
question for the owner.

## Cooling-off read

Before the final sign-off, leave the report, re-read the reasoning
journal, then read the report again. The gap catches assumptions that
were invisible while drafting and wording that only made sense to the
author.

## Review record

Keep the review record with the report package:

- lenses selected and why;
- reviewer names or roles;
- exceptions and their disposition;
- residual disagreements;
- sign-off by the accountable reviewer.

A review that leaves no record is indistinguishable from no review.

## Adaptation points

- Which lenses are mandatory in the methodology?
- Who is independent enough to review each lens?
- What result scale is used?
- Which exceptions block release, and which are advisory?
- How are disagreements escalated?
